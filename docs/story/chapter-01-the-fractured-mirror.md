# Chapter 1: The Fractured Mirror

Morning in New Elysium arrived not with dawn, but with the abrupt illumination of countless screens flickering to life. Clayton Harrow awoke to the sterile buzz of his alarm, the digital display casting a cold glow across his sparsely furnished bedroom.

Another day. Another loop.

He moved through his apartment with mechanical precision—shower, dress, coffee. Black. No sugar. The bitterness was familiar, grounding. Headlines scrolled across his tablet: markets, politics, scandals. Noise without meaning.

A flicker crossed the screen.

For a fraction of a second, the text dissolved into interlocking circles and lines—the same symbols he had seen the night before. He blinked. Normal again.

Probably a glitch.

Outside, the city roared to life. Magnetic rails hummed, holographic advertisements promised a brighter future, and Nexum Enterprises loomed above it all—mirrored, immaculate, inhuman.

At work, hours slipped by unnoticed. Contracts. Negotiations. Empty conversations. During a meeting, the presentation screen flickered again. Symbols flashed beneath the graphs.

“Did anyone see that?” Clayton asked.

Janine looked confused. “See what?”

He let it go.

At lunch, he wandered beyond the corporate grid. The streets narrowed. The buildings aged. The air felt heavier.

Then it began to rain.

Except the raindrops never fell.

They froze in midair, shimmering. The alley dissolved. Brick became water. Fish swam through the air, luminous and impossible. Jellyfish drifted overhead, trailing threads of light.

Clayton stood paralyzed, reality unraveling around him.

Then—impact.

The world snapped back into place. The alley was empty. Dry. Ordinary.

Clayton collapsed against the wall, gasping.

“I think I’m losing my mind,” he whispered.

That evening, in the break room at Nexum, he met Seamus O’Driscoll.

“You ever get the feeling,” Seamus asked quietly, “that things aren’t quite what they seem?”

Clayton looked at him, heart pounding.

“Yes,” he said. “Every day.”
